## Red flags
- Asks you for money, a fee, a "deposit" or a paid service before or instead of investing.
- Her main business is selling sourcing, factory or sample services: then she is a vendor, not an angel, and her advice is conflicted.
- Cannot name one past investment or one founder you can call; vague on fund size or source.
- Pushes speed: sign now, wire now, move to another platform now, meet a factory next week.
- Wants equity, a commission or exclusivity in exchange for introductions.
- Asks for your designs or supplier details and offers no verifiable credentials in return.

## ARESA contract points to check (by Fri 4 Dec, D-018) (unverified, from general Spanish labour law; a lawyer or the contract text decides)
- Pluriempleo or exclusive dedication clause: does outside work need prior written authorization? Does exclusivity come with the compensation the law expects?
- Non-competition during the contract (Estatuto de los Trabajadores, art. 21 as I recall): does it apply to consumer gadgets, and is there an employer interest that justifies it?
- Post-contract non-compete: length and compensation.
- Employee inventions (Ley de Patentes 24/2015, invention provisions as I recall) and any IP-assignment clause: who owns something designed outside hours but touching skills or tools learnt at work?
- Confidentiality clause: scope, and whether it blocks you naming the employer.
- Use of company time, equipment and contacts: none, in writing, for this project.
- Collective agreement (convenio colectivo) for ARESA's sector: extra duties on outside activity or inventions.
- Does founding a company or taking investment need disclosure to the employer, and is there a notice period if you later leave?
- The coffee itself is a conversation; the risk starts when you sign, take money or build on the employer's time.
