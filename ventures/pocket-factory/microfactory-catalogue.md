## 3. Products and prices

### 3.1 Current line-up (site, read 13 Nov 2026)

From [microfactory.com](https://microfactory.com/). Positioning: "Robotic cells for high-mix, low-volume tasks with a built-in AI integrator."

| Product | Starting price | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| MicroFactory Single | $11,950 | 1x Fairino FR3 industrial arm, 1 hot-swap gripper, 1 active stereo camera, control box, optical breadboard, vision alignment light panel, box frame |
| MicroFactory Double | $21,900 | 2x Fairino FR3, 2 grippers, 2 stereo cameras, same base kit |
| MicroFactory Dev Kit | $31,900 | Same hardware as Double, sold as an "Open platform with no vendor lock-in" |

Terms on the same page:
- Customers pay Anthropic or OpenAI tokens for training and reconfiguring. A 5-year licence for local models is included, so running a configured task has no usage fee.
- Data goes to Cloudflare (storage), Modal (training) and Fly.io (fleet management). Inference runs locally, and a fully local setup is possible by agreement.
- 30-day money-back guarantee after delivery. A queue spot needs a "fully refundable $50 deposit."
- Arms supported today: Fairino. Others on request.

Specs: 160 x 90 x 85 cm, 80 kg, 3 kg payload, 622 mm reach, 6 axes, 0.02 mm pose repeatability (ISO 9283), 1 m/s typical TCP speed, 30,000 hour expected lifetime.

Four task types the AI integrator handles:
1. Classical vision automation (bin picking, connector insertion).
2. Tasks that need reasoning during execution, where the model controls the robot directly. The site says this "Can be slow."
3. Complex repetitive motions recorded with the teleoperation kit.
4. Deformable, high-variability objects (zip ties, wires), handled by collecting data and setting up continual learning.

- May 2025: "Two arms, cameras, a box-frame, and a teleoperation kit" at "5K per robot" ([ihorbeaver-sweep.md section 4](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1928176075080642884)).
- Sep 2025: "priced at around $5,000," desktop size, "paid reservations from over 100 customers," first units expected early 2026, long-term hardware cost "below $1,000 per unit" ([Automation News](https://automationnews.com/solutions/robotics-motion/affordable-general-purpose-robot-for-manufacturing/)). Igor's own aim then: "charge $5k for two arms and a box in mass production" ([23 Sep 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1970536676444512357)).
- Nov 2025: DevKit, "the first production version," with custom arms ([7 Nov 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1986859432165405179)).
- Jul 2026: move to off-the-shelf arms ([Viktor, 26 Jul 2026](https://x.com/viktor_vrp/status/2081244266303717778)).

### 3.3 Software and models

- **Loop Model 1**: data-efficient learning model; "20x more throughput per unit of data than 'Pi06 + RLT'" on zip-tie insertion ([12 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2054253758406910457), 77k views).
- **Customer self-onboarding** with tutorials and chat support ([28 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2060073842799002054)); Viktor called it a key milestone ([13 May 2026](https://x.com/viktor_vrp/status/2054417565154521467)).

- **AI robotic integrator**, an agent "Powered by the latest Astra and Opus models" that "designs tooling, writes computer vision code, and guides a human operator" ([28 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2104646736652447854)).

After the Fable 5.2 launch Igor said they have "had it deployed for a month now" and offered production tips ([9 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2119911098564612200)).

## 5. Customers and deployments

- **Unruggable** (San Francisco smart-wallet startup; founders named as Adam and Bill). Paid $20K for a MicroFactory to automate assembly. Reported details:
  - Unruggable had 1,000+ preorders, and the founders spent 2 of 6 months assembling by hand.
  - The cell runs "nearly 24/7" with a part-time, non-technical operator reloading materials.
  - They redesigned the device for robot assembly (snap fits, feeder-friendly screws).
  - They plan to assemble live at a November trade show.
  - They say the robot also helped with marketing and investor pitching.
  - Source: [case study](https://case-study.microfactory.com/). This is the "customer deployment" behind Igor's Oct and Nov 2026 posts. The $20K matches the two-arm price.
- **Preorders.** "Hundreds of preorders," including electronics assembly and "processing snails to be shipped to France for escargot" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/16/this-30m-startup-built-a-dog-crate-sized-robot-factory-that-learns-by-watching-humans/)). "Paid reservations from over 100 customers" in electronics, textiles, food processing and lab automation ([Automation News](https://automationnews.com/solutions/robotics-motion/affordable-general-purpose-robot-for-manufacturing/)). No public figure for how many reservations converted to deliveries was found.
- **Deliveries and other work.**
  - "Today's local client delivery" ([Viktor, 31 Jul 2026](https://x.com/viktor_vrp/status/2083115299872850398)).
  - An arm "retiring from food processing" to move to electronics assembly, "From mushroom scooper to Mr. Dexterous Grasping" ([7 Aug 2026](https://x.com/viktor_vrp/status/2085561442158452830); [9 Aug 2026](https://x.com/viktor_vrp/status/2086303380683759633)).
  - A customer self-onboarding ([28 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2060073842799002054)).
  - A customer magnet-stack task ([4 Jun 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2062332769863438696), in the earlier sweep).
- **Earliest real-product demo.** A product "previously assembled by an offshore factory," built for a client ([Automation News](https://automationnews.com/solutions/robotics-motion/affordable-general-purpose-robot-for-manufacturing/)), likely the photo frame ([2 Nov 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1984781192647024670)).
- **Current sales ask** (from the earlier sweep): data-centre components, lab automation, on-demand assembly ([6 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118842770660167825)).

## 11. Strengths, weaknesses, and what they have not done (opinion)

Everything in this section is my opinion, built on the facts above.

**Strengths.**
- They have a paying customer in production and a public case study with numbers. That is the exact evidence the investor sources in `sim-demos-scan.md` section 3.4 ask for.
- Their stack rides frontier models (System 2) plus model-written code (System 1). Every new model release improves their product for free, and Igor is already marketing against Fable 5.2.
- Distribution: one viral video, top-tier angels, Founders Inc, and a founder who writes clearly. Their story is "re-industrialise America" and "self-replicating factory," the same ladder Oscar wants.
- They are pragmatic: they abandoned custom arms for off-the-shelf industrial arms to ship faster, and they use $300 feeders and jigs instead of clever grasps.

**Weaknesses.**
- The team is very small (3 on public profiles) with $1.61M raised and no announced round since Sep 2025.
- The price moved far from the "$5K robot" that made them famous. At $11,950 to $31,900 on Fairino arms, they now compete with ordinary cobot integrators on price, and their differentiator is the AI integrator software.
- Public reach has dropped since the launch.
- Reported throughput is modest (1 gadget per 7 minutes), and reliability numbers come from their own posts.
- Their own cofounder says simulation does not work well for their precise tasks, so they have no sim story to offer investors.

**What they have not done, as far as public sources show.**
- No raw-stock processing: no machining, cutting, casting, welding or forming in the cell. All public work is assembly of bought-in parts, plus soldering demos.
- No self-replication demonstrated. It is a stated goal with a manifesto.
- No container, remote-site or off-grid deployment.
- No published simulation, digital twin or virtual demo of the product.
- No certifications, uptime statistics across several customers, or units-shipped figures.
- No open-source hardware or public developer ecosystem around the Dev Kit, beyond the "no vendor lock-in" claim.
- No company X account in use, and no public job postings.

**What this means for Pocket Factory (opinion).** The open lane is raw stock to finished part, plus a credible simulated version of it. That is exactly what MicroFactory lacks. Their customer evidence also sets the bar Oscar will be compared against once he talks to hardware investors.
