# @ihorbeaver sweep, for Pocket Factory

Written 13 Nov 2026 (Europe/Madrid). Read-only X sweep of [Igor Kulakov](https://x.com/ihorbeaver), founder of [MicroFactory](https://microfactory.com/). No posts, likes, follows, replies or DMs were made. Every claim below cites a post URL or, for product facts not posted as tweets, the company site. No formal SWOT post was found on the account; strategy is taken from the posts that play that role.

## 1. Who he is and what he builds

[Igor Kulakov](https://x.com/ihorbeaver) (user id 10489332) is in San Francisco. The account was created on 23 Nov 2007. As of this sweep his profile listed 10,466 followers, 1,395 posts, and the bio "Making physical products too cheap to meter." The site in the profile is [microfactory.com](https://microfactory.com/).

Before the robot, he described himself as someone who could ship a product in a day, had raised $100k on Kickstarter, and had worked as a game designer and indie game developer, already using LLMs and Whisper ([19 Jul 2023](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1681774330810712065)). In 2023 the timeline is mostly software: a Twitter engagement extension, a language-practice app, a note-taking prototype, and an open-source wearable AI kit built from a Raspberry board, battery, mic and speaker, "no soldering needed," at about $40 to $60 in parts ([23 Dec 2023](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1738675656454484467)).

The robotics line starts in public on 1 Jul 2024, with a toy arm simulation and the line "In the future it will be two-arms robot for assembly lines. It can screw, solder, glue, connect body parts" ([1 Jul 2024](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1807631612043305368)). He 3D-printed an arm's parts ([24 Nov 2024](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1860539046310871134)), assembled it and immediately redrew it after real tests ([4 Dec 2024](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1864429517064032295)), posted a soldering video as "the last piece of the puzzle for electronics assembly" after already showing screwing, stickers, packing and unpacking ([8 Feb 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1888344864208908357)), and in a reply described the product as "A microfactory the size of a washing machine. You throw materials into it, robo-arms inside solder, screw and assemble the product" ([8 Mar 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1898431584686194904)).

By late 2025 he was talking about a production arm that "Can work 24/7," is "10 times more precise" and "3 times more powerful" than the demo, and easier to assemble ([17 Sep 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1968371680780370358)), still aiming to "charge $5k for two arms and a box in mass production" ([23 Sep 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1970536676444512357)). He changed his bio to "Building self-replicating robots" and wrote that if the robot assembles electronics, "the obvious first use case is to assemble copies of itself" ([5 Oct 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1974689269068579144)). He launched the MicroFactory DevKit as "a general-purpose robot designed to automate manual work. With a glimpse of self-replication" ([7 Nov 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1986859432165405179)), showed a full autonomous run assembling an Amazon-sold photo frame ([2 Nov 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1984781192647024670)), put a second unit in a line ("Real-world Factorio is coming") ([18 Feb 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2023982250594029682)), released Loop Model 1 ([12 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2054253758406910457)), shipped an interchangeable gripper ([17 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2078186004192911387)), introduced an AI robotic integrator driven by Astra and Opus ([28 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2104646736652447854)), and by mid-October had "completely switched to frontier LLMs to control a robot in a customer deployment" ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866012333838675)).

The current product, on the company site, is a boxed robotic cell for high-mix, low-volume work, with a built-in AI integrator (Anthropic and OpenAI), hot-swappable grippers, and Fairino FR3 arms. Listed starting prices on 13 Nov 2026: single-arm cell $11,950, two-arm cell $21,900, Dev Kit $31,900. Specs on that page: 160 x 90 x 85 cm, 80 kg, 3 kg payload, 622 mm reach, 6 axes, 0.02 mm pose repeatability, 30,000 hour expected lifetime ([microfactory.com](https://microfactory.com/)).

## 2. Top 10 takeaways for Pocket Factory

1. His public pitch is already close to Pocket Factory: a washing-machine sized box you throw materials into, with arms that solder, screw and assemble ([8 Mar 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1898431584686194904)).
2. The stated endgame is not a soldering cell. It is a self-replicating factory that "will build humanoids, enable the US reindustrialization, and help colonise other planets as a side product" ([5 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2107210345051988131)), and he has already decided that if the cell assembles electronics, "the obvious first use case is to assemble copies of itself" ([5 Oct 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1974689269068579144)).
3. This is not only a demo account. In a customer deployment he says they replaced all operations with frontier-LLM control, ran a gadget assembly for 1.5 hours with no errors ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866629714469204)), and later showed a 2.5 hour autonomous run at one gadget per 7 minutes, stopping only when parts ran out ([6 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118842309156741404)).
4. What works today, in his own limitation note, is LLM-written code in a constrained box for repetitive electronics assembly. It is "not so suitable for non-repetitive work like cleaning a house, since direct LLM control is slow and unreliable" ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866867586007326)). "Build anything, given tools and materials" is the ambition, not the current method.
5. He now says there are "no technical bottlenecks" to a v1 self-replicating factory that does final assembly from off-the-shelf components, because a current customer task already needs "a similar level of precision to assembling a robotic arm" ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866870215782551)).
6. The AI stack he is standardising on is System 2 plus System 1: an Opus/Astra (and sometimes Claude) agent for reasoning, tooling and code, and computer-vision code or a small local model for fast, editable, reliable motion. He calls this "the right side of the Bitter Lesson" and says those frontier models are "many generations more capable than any VLA" at the high-level layer ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866012333838675); Claude example, [30 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2105089285892477010)).
7. Reliability, for him, is corner-case handling, not a perfect setup: a PCB that bounces onto an edge gets a programmed push; buttons that sit too close get split; a random pile gets a flip platform; screws get a $300 feeder instead of a clever grasp ([6 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118842708731298082); [15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866708798046444); [15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866864092143973)).
8. Hardware quality is treated as a requirement for the AI to work. Harmonic, industrial internals that do not wobble let him run the model at 3x teleop speed (180 fps vs 60) ([13 Jan 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2010971701220426188)). He asked another builder if they plan to use harmonic motors because "We need harmonic motors for our model to work" ([3 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2073186007718256669)). A production arm is described as 24/7-capable, "10 times more precise" and "3 times more powerful" than the demo ([17 Sep 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1968371680780370358)). He answers a question about cheap arms with "No. It's industrial arms, and they are designed to work 24/7 under high loads" ([7 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118859710690668751)).
9. His go-to-market is dense manufacturing hubs and high-mix, low-volume work, not a remote village first: "we need to re-industrialize the Bay Area," with Shenzhen as the hardware analogue ([27 Jul 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1949575324850934176)); a thought experiment of filling Salesforce Tower with cells "could replace Shenzhen for the US" ([20 Feb 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2024693339455705550)); current sales ask is data-center components, lab automation, or on-demand assembly ([6 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118842770660167825)); after initial setup, "switching tasks takes 5 minutes" ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110867810859815017)). He did, separately, agree that "a self-contained small factory in a container sounds like a good product" ([18 Mar 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2034357121438126295)).
10. Speed of iteration is real, and it cuts both ways. He went from a toy simulation in Jul 2024 to a production DevKit, a named model, an AI integrator, and a customer deployment in about 15 months (see the posts in section 1). A formal SWOT write-up was not found anywhere on the account.

## 3. SWOT or strategy points

No post on this account is titled SWOT, and a search for SWOT, "best practice" and similar terms returned no hits. The strategy that is actually written down is in the posts below.

**Strengths he claims, or demonstrates.**

- The box form factor plus repetitive electronics assembly is a setting where LLM-written code is "fast, editable, and reliable" ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866012333838675); [15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866867586007326)).
- Frontier-model agents already design tooling, write vision code, collect small datasets, and step in when a new situation appears, which he treats as in-context and continual learning for free ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866012333838675); [28 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2104646736652447854)).
- Data efficiency: Loop Model 1, on zip-tie insertion, is claimed at "20x more throughput per unit of data than 'Pi06 + RLT' from Physical Intelligence," and is "the missing piece" because "deployment becomes so simple and fast that our users can do it themselves" ([12 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2054253758406910457)). A later blog note on cable-tie tightening says their pipeline used 10x less data and reached close to 100% reliability, against 5 to 10 hours of data and close to 85% for π0.6 plus an RL token ([Continual learning is all you need](https://microfactory.com/articles/continual-learning), 2 Aug 2026). Other public figures: a precision test "Trained with 2 minutes examples" ([30 Jun 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2072001151730606531)); "100% from 16 examples" on a task segment ([1 Aug 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2083669346552103036)); a VLA picking a small PCB at any pose after 35 demonstrations, "5 mins in total," "if you run them on good hardware" ([20 Jan 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2013466973007425936)); "8-10 hours of training" for "commercially useful tasks" and a worker that "reliably does the job 24/7 for years" ([7 Jan 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2008989655199584647)).
- Task changeover after setup is quoted at 5 minutes, and a filmed "automate an assembly task in 5 minutes" demo is framed as proof that "We don’t need to wait for some distant future" ([16 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2077805676274016279); [15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110867810859815017)).
- Customers have been shown self-onboarding, collecting data and training Loop Model from a tutorial, with chat support ([28 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2060073842799002054)).

**Weaknesses and limits he admits.**

- Direct LLM control is "slow and unreliable" outside a constrained, repetitive cell. House-cleaning is his counter-example ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866867586007326)).
- He expected to automate only 90% of operations on a customer gadget and then replaced all of them, but still notes suction cups would have been "faster and easier" than the dexterous grippers they kept ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866629714469204)).
- The site itself says to start simple, that "Prompts matter," that some tasks still need human input or are "too complex for it yet," and offers a 30-day money-back period ([microfactory.com](https://microfactory.com/)).
- Early arm design: shake, not datasheet "preciseness" or "repeatability," was what made USB insertion hard; joint placement was wrong; they spent another week redrawing the arm from scratch ([4 Dec 2024](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1864429517064032295)).
- Off-the-shelf robots create "O(n) manufacturing pain" the moment you change something as small as a gripper, including repackaging, shipments and storage ([6 Aug 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1953182941405954343)). (The current cells still ship with Fairino FR3 arms, per the site, so this is a design principle, not a claim that every arm is in-house.)

**Opportunities he is pointing at.**

- Self-replication by assembling a new cell from off-the-shelf parts, coordinated by an AI agent that specialises cells with fixtures and tools for assembly, calibration, testing, 3D printing and so on. "Sounds like sci-fi, but recent releases of Astra/Opus can coordinate robots and control them by writing reliable code. That makes this already possible" ([5 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2107210345051988131)).
- Reindustrialisation has to beat economic laws, not just automate existing factories. He cites "Only 30-40k robots were installed in the US last year" as evidence that "the pull from existing factories is weak," and says humanoids are not a self-build solution because "who will build them? Still humans" ([5 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2107210345051988131)).
- High-mix, low-volume work, plus "a personal robo-factory powered by local AI," because "Local language models aren’t smart enough to create real economic value just by working 24/7" on their own ([3 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2073154350483157264)).
- Science labs: "Standardized replicable cells, industrial grade precise arms, interchangeable grippers for tool use" ([8 Feb 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2020610122293359038)).
- A container-scale factory, which he endorsed in a reply even though it is not the current SKU ([18 Mar 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2034357121438126295)).
- Units in a line, not a single hero cell ([18 Feb 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2023982250594029682)).

**Threats and competitive posture.**

- Humanoid labs are the public alternative he argues against, on self-build and on tool use: "it’s hard to pick up a tool firmly and control precisely with them" ([17 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2078186004192911387); [5 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2107210345051988131)).
- Large VLA or foundation-model labs are the comparison set (Physical Intelligence is the named benchmark for Loop Model 1) ([12 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2054253758406910457)). His counter is data efficiency, self-serve deployment, and riding frontier LLMs instead of training a general robot brain.
- Shenzhen-scale labour is the manufacturing comparison; his answer is a dense bay of small cells next to AI talent and a port, not a village site ([27 Jul 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1949575324850934176); [20 Feb 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2024693339455705550)).

## 4. Good practices for building hardware and robots fast

- Prove the task with teleoperation first. "If it can be done in this way, it can eventually be done with the model" ([29 Apr 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1917322706338603032)). The site's loop is: show the motion, nudge in real time when it misses, then chain operations ([microfactory.com/backup](https://microfactory.com/backup)).
- Use real tasks as the yardstick, not datasheet precision. USB-connector insertion exposed shake that "preciseness" and "repeatability" numbers did not; they redrew the arm and made that insertion the reference task for the next iteration ([4 Dec 2024](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1864429517064032295)).
- After a failed layout, redraw from scratch in a week rather than patching. Joint placement matters as much as the number of degrees of freedom: "it's better to have side rotations closer to the gripper" ([4 Dec 2024](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1864429517064032295)).
- Get rid of shake before you scale. The production-arm launch post leads with "Finally completely got rid of all shakings" and quiet harmonic motors ([23 Sep 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1970536676444512357)).
- Constrain the environment on purpose: a box-frame, cameras, consistent lighting, an optical breadboard. Fixtures inside the box can stand in for a second hand. "The arm also has an interchangeable gripper. So quite a lot can be done with just one arm" ([6 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2107369078545658356)). A May 2025 kit description was "Two arms, cameras, a box-frame, and a teleoperation kit" at "5K per robot" ([29 May 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1928174527424458840); [29 May 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1928176075080642884)).
- Build jigs for the awkward step, rather than hoping the policy is perfect: an extra platform to flip a piled part ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866708798046444)); "Strong magnets can make the environment self-resetting" ([29 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2060455103610622091)).
- Buy a cheap standard tool when it exists. A $300 automatic screw feeder is preferred to a clever grasp of loose screws ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866864092143973)).
- Swap the whole tool-fixed gripper rather than trying to hold tools with a humanoid hand. "The tool is fixed to the gripper in advance, and the whole hand is swapped. AI is quite good at generating this kind of custom tooling" ([17 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2078186004192911387)).
- If you need customisation, design the robot yourself from the motors up, because changing an off-the-shelf arm is still O(n) work, "but now at least it's tailored exactly to your needs" ([6 Aug 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1953182941405954343)).
- For complex work before you have a large robotics dataset, decompose the task and film the full run, as in the Amazon photo-frame assembly ([2 Nov 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1984781192647024670)).
- Let the customer deploy. Loop Model 1 is pitched so "users can do it themselves" ([12 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2054253758406910457); [28 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2060073842799002054)).
- Recipe for "near-100% success rates" that "unlocks real business applications": "Step 1: Imitation learning. Step 2: Adjusting movements. Step 3: Self-improving" ([18 Nov 2025](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/1990652473422586338)).
- Treat bare imitation learning, without robotic pre-training, as a first-class method: "reliable, predictable, easy to modify and debug," with "a small dataset instead of code," "glued together by a vision encoder and a transformer." He compares it to how Claude Code needed years of hardened production code before it could exist ([12 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2076338101321187637)).

## 5. AI tips and workflows

- Put a frontier LLM in the loop as the integrator, not only as a planner. The agent "designs tooling, writes computer vision code, and guides a human operator, taking care of all the boring parts of hardware production" ([28 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2104646736652447854)).
- Prefer code the model writes over the model driving the arm directly, once the task is repetitive. In October he was "switching our customer’s setup from direct model control to control by code written by Astra/Opus," including using a light panel as a mask for precise insertion ([2 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2105901255893885064)).
- When a problem shows up, "the agent steps in, edits the code to handle it, and adds a verifications" (wording as posted). Example pipeline for buttons in a pile: grasp one or two, drop on a platform, toss until the side is right, separate if they sit too close ([28 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2104648248237625737); later the same pattern in production, [6 Nov 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118842767308927110)).
- For precise insertion, have the LLM write code that collects a small dataset, then train a linear regression to align the part from visual feedback "during thousands of runs." Claude was shown doing "a little dance" to collect that set ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866831384953224); [30 Sep 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2105089285892477010)).
- Use a reliable VLA when it removes tooling: a customer magnet-stack task is done in the grippers, "No coding, except saving points," at 1x speed, autonomous, with the model finding height by eye and correcting slight randomness ([4 Jun 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2062332769863438696)).
- VLAs "can generalize pretty well from very little data, if you run them on good hardware" ([20 Jan 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2013466973007425936)). The hardware clause is doing work in that sentence.
- "Deployment first models. Whether it has VLA or WAM as a backbone, it should learn after deployment" ([8 May 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2052891103326781622)).
- On stiff industrial arms, raise control rate rather than slowing the task: 3x teleop FPS ([13 Jan 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2010971701220426188)). He later says you can "usually run the model reliably at 2x/3x the speed of teleoperation. Depends on the precision that is needed" ([1 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2072157402204705235)).
- Local models do the repeated execution; frontier models do setup, repair of the pipeline, and reconfiguration. The site says you pay Anthropic or OpenAI tokens for training and reconfiguring, then run local models under a 5-year license with no usage fee ([microfactory.com](https://microfactory.com/)). His Jul 2026 post is the same idea in one line: local LLMs alone are not enough, "a personal robo-factory powered by local AI" is ([3 Jul 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2073154350483157264)).
- Stay on the Bitter Lesson: benefit from "the billions being invested in frontier models around the world" rather than betting the company on a custom generalist policy ([15 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2110866012333838675)).
