Written 2026-11-13 by the evening HQ run (task 2026-11-13-003, filed by Innovator from Oscar's 16:37 direction: the Pocket Factory path decision, no spend, idea then market map then pitch deck then investor). Read-only research: nothing was bought, contacted, signed up for or published. Sources: `landscape.md`, `ihorbeaver-sweep.md`, `microfactory-catalogue.md`, `sim-demos-scan.md`, `plan.md` in this folder, plus five parallel web searches run tonight (robot cells, expeditionary fabrication and buyers, desktop fabrication, robot foundation models, market figures). ## 1. The question

Who sells what today for a factory-in-a-box that turns raw stock into finished parts where there is no supply chain: at what price, funded by whom and how much, bought by whom, and where the gap is.

What the buyer evidence says: the only dollar-denominated purchases of a complete deployable factory in 2024 to 2026 are US DoD and tied to drones (Firestorm). Spare-parts buyers (US Navy, USMC, Royal Navy) buy individual printers in the 89,500 to 203,210 USD class plus training, not containers, and they run them with trained sailors. Commercial maritime and energy buyers have organised digital part libraries and print-on-demand services at hubs, not at the point of need. Mining: no site programme with a published figure was found.

## 4. The gap, in plain words

1. Robot cells assemble parts someone else made (MicroFactory, Almond, Foundry, Bright Machines, Formic, Sunrise). Only Machina Labs turns raw metal into parts, and it does so at factory scale with 124M USD behind it.
2. Fabrication machines make parts but have no hands: no desktop printer, laser or CNC on the market loads its own stock, moves the part to the next step or checks the result. A person does that.
3. Deployable fabrication exists and is paid for, but every fielded container or shelter needs trained people inside: two engineers in the Army lab, a 15-day course for SPEE3D, 1.5 weeks for the USMC machinists, QinetiQ staff at Clyde for a year.
4. The brain to replace that person has arrived in the last 13 months (AMP1, AA-Robotics, Gemini 4 Argon, frontier-model control in MicroFactory's production) and is rented by the token, but no one has put it in front of a fabrication machine and shipped the result.

The empty square: a boxed cell where the agent runs the fabrication machines and the arms move the part between them, from a text request to an accepted part, with no skilled operator, priced in the fab-lab class (25,000 to 120,000 USD) rather than the Army-container class (2.8M USD). MicroFactory's founder names the same endgame and could add a printer module quickly (ihorbeaver-sweep.md), so the gap is open but not reserved.

## 5. Three facts a pitch deck should lead with

1. The demand is paid for and documented. The US Navy's FY2026 budget carries a 196.585M USD line for afloat and deployable manufacturing (https://www.highergov.com/budget/navy-advanced-manufacturing-5483f47); APFIT has awarded over 1.5B USD since 2022, 30M USD of it in May 2026 for five containerised print cells (https://www.defensedaily.com/dod-awards-first-round-of-apfit-projects-using-fy-26-funds/pentagon/; https://www.tectonicdefense.com/exclusive-firestorm-snags-30m-apfit-contract/); at RIMPAC 2026 a deployable hybrid cell made 12 brackets for 2,492 USD against a 9,624 USD shipyard quote (https://www.tctmagazine.com/snowbird-technology-reports-cost-savings-on-4-parts-3d-printed-during-rimpac-2026-exercise/).
2. Every deployable factory sold today ships with people inside. The Army lab needed two engineers (https://singularityhub.com/2013/02/28/3d-printing-on-the-frontlines-army-deploying-2-8m-mobile-fabrication-labs); SPEE3D's Ukrainian operators took a 15-day course (https://www.spee3d.com/metal-parts-on-the-frontlines-spee3ds-impact-on-ukrainian-military-operations/); the Fab Foundation's 1,750 labs prove the machines travel and the skilled operator does not (https://fabfoundation.org/getting-started).
3. The operator just became software. Frontier models now drive real arms zero-shot (Apple AMP1, 85.6 percent on RoboLab-120 against 49.2 for the best trained robot policy, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892), their robot-control ability doubled every 53.9 days from July to October 2026 (https://lesswrong.com/posts/ndS9Xn8Qx5PppHJ3Q/the-ability-of-frontier-models-to-control-robots-is-rapidly), and a three-person company already runs customer production on frontier-model-written code for hours at a time (https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2118842309156741404). Nobody has yet pointed that operator at a fabrication machine and sold the box.

The honest caveats for the same slide: frontier-model control is slow (about 30 minutes per task against 20 seconds for a human, https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/aa-robotics); the closest competitor says the model needs stiff industrial arms and that simulation does not model precise assembly well (https://x.com/ihorbeaver/status/2073186007718256669; https://x.com/viktor_vrp/status/2080824882582151569); and the buyers with budgets are defence buyers, which brings export control and procurement rules the regulation-procurement role has not mapped yet.
