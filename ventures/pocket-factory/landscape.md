# Pocket Factory: landscape scan

Written 13 Nov 2026 (Europe/Madrid) by Cofounder. Read-only research: nothing was posted, followed, liked, messaged or bought.
Source idea: `transcript-2026-11-13.md` in this folder.
Method: X public search (full archive, posts since May 2026), web search and fetch, GitHub, Hacker News, arXiv, YC launch pages. Every web link was checked with curl on 13 Nov. A few news sites (Business Wire, GlobeNewswire, Axios) block scripted fetches with 403, so I used another source where one existed. X post dates are UTC.
No numbers below are estimates. Every figure is quoted from the linked source, and claims in a company's own press release are its claims, not verified facts.

## 1. Summary (5 lines)

1. Your exact product already exists for assembly: MicroFactory (San Francisco) sells a boxed one- or two-arm cell with hot-swap grippers and a built-in "AI integrator" running Anthropic and OpenAI models, from $11,950 ([site](https://microfactory.com/)).
2. Its bigger cousins work at factory scale: Foundry Robotics ($100M Series A), Hadrian ($1.37B Series D), Machina Labs ($124M Series C), Bright Machines, Almond's dual-arm Axol, and Theker in Barcelona ($85M Series A).
3. What changed in the last 30 days: general LLMs now drive arms zero-shot better than trained robot policies. Apple's AMP1 scored 85.6% on RoboLab-120 against 49.2% for the best VLA ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892)). Claude Fable 5.2 leads Artificial Analysis's real-arm benchmark at 75 ([Ridge](https://www.ridgebench.com/benchmarks/aa-robotics)), but these runs take about 30 minutes per task against 20 seconds for a human ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/aa-robotics)).
4. Still open: turning raw material into a finished part on one bench. Multi-tool machines like the Bambu H2D fabricate but have no arms, the arm cells assemble parts you supply, and the open "civilisation kit" efforts (Open Source Ecology) are still mostly in development.
5. The likely gap is an open, cheap, LLM-run bench that goes from a text request to a made or repaired part, with a reusable recipe library (your STC). The one field signal I found says the first real demand is spare and repair parts at the point of need, not new products ([Firestorm Labs](https://x.com/Firestorm_Labs/status/2090046283088380365)).

## 4. Candidate niches (learnable fast, frontier reachable)

1. **Agentic LLM control of cheap arms for bench tasks.** The frontier moved in the last month (AA-Robotics launched 14 Oct 2026, AMP1 posted 6 Nov 2026), so nobody has years of lead. It is mostly software and agent design, which you already do daily. The open problem is speed and reliability: about 30 minutes per task against 20 seconds for a human ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/aa-robotics)). MicroFactory's answer is to use the frontier model for setup and small local models at runtime ([site](https://microfactory.com/)).
   First step: get one SO-101 (a builder lists $122 per arm and $230 for the leader and follower pair, [post](https://x.com/0x_meden/status/2110459288779182265)), run Quackd or the AMP1 harness, and reproduce two AA-Robotics tasks. Publish times and scores.
2. **LLM-driven soldering and PCB rework.** It is narrow and measurable (a joint passes or fails), and it is your transcript's own first use case. Real money is moving in it (Theker in Barcelona, Circuex, Tuurny), but nobody I found does it on a cheap open bench with an LLM in the loop. Space and remote repair need it too (Tuurny's NASA-funded grant).
   First step: put a soldering iron on a 3D-printer gantry or an SO-101 with a camera, and have an agent solder a through-hole practice kit. Log the pass rate per joint.
3. **Tool changing and tool use for low-cost arms.** "Two arms that connect to any tool" is the heart of the idea. The parts exist for industrial arms (MicroFactory hot-swap grippers, wireless magnetic changers like [MAGBOT](https://x.com/Und_Global/status/2102060892078911807)), but hobby builders are still hand-rolling ROS2 attach and detach ([post](https://x.com/demon4eg/status/2065442682055397747)).
   First step: design a passive magnetic tool dock for the SO-101 (gripper, screwdriver, soldering iron), then have an agent pick the right tool by name.
4. **Text-to-part fabrication loop (the raw-material side).** Request, then CAD, then print or cut on a Bambu H2D-class machine, then an arm unloads and finishes the part. Nobody in the table closes this loop on a desk. This is the STC as a recipe library, and it is where Firestorm says demand lives (repair parts at the edge).
   First step: have an agent generate a replacement part (a bracket or knob) as CAD from a photo and dimensions, print it, and record success. Add the arm later.
5. **Eval and test benches for physical agents.** The benchmarks are new and small (AA-Robotics has eight tasks and one arm). RoboColosseum offers 10,000 free evaluation hours ([post](https://x.com/b__slicklein/status/2118006202936274984)). A public bench for "workshop tasks" (cut, solder, screw) would put you at the frontier fast, and it doubles as your product's test suite.
   First step: write 5 workshop tasks with pass criteria in AA-Robotics format and run them on niche 1's setup.

Top 3 for you: niches 1, 2 and 3, in that order. Niche 1 is the base that 2 and 3 sit on.

## 5. Ideas to steal

- **AI integrator, not AI operator** (MicroFactory): the frontier model designs the tooling, writes the vision code and prints fixtures, then hands the repeat work to small local models, so tokens are only spent on setup ([site](https://microfactory.com/)).
- **Put it in a box** (MicroFactory): a fixed frame with fixed cameras and a light panel makes training easier and data more transferable ([Automation News](https://automationnews.com/solutions/robotics-motion/affordable-general-purpose-robot-for-manufacturing/)).
- **Pre-sell before shipping**: MicroFactory took 100+ paid reservations before its first units shipped (same source). It also offers a 30-day money-back guarantee.
- **The STC is memory**: in AMP1, a memory store carried across episodes cut bimanual failures 4.6x ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892)). Your "all of humanity's knowledge" kit is that store, shipped with the box.
- **Bare test interface**: AA-Robotics gives a model only an HTTP API, four cameras and a submit call ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/aa-robotics)). Build your bench to that interface from day one.
- **Repair before creation**: Firestorm found the real demand was repair parts, not new drones ([post](https://x.com/Firestorm_Labs/status/2090046283088380365)). Pitch the remote and space use case as "never wait for a spare part".
- **Robot builds the robot**: Almond uses Axol to work on parts for Axol ([post](https://x.com/IlirAliu_/status/2102307411470012615)). MicroFactory started with electronics because the robot can build robots. It is a strong STC story and real dogfooding.
- **Own the factory, sell output** (Foundry): manufacturing as a service lets every run feed the models ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/foundry-robotics-raises-100m-series-a-to-build-autonomous-factory-for-assembly)). It is a fallback model if selling boxes is slow.
- **Safety gate before motion**: fixed rules check each LLM plan before the arm moves ([@rushikesh_84](https://x.com/rushikesh_84/status/2117819102584856659)). Prompt-injection risk on arm agents is now benchmarked ([post](https://x.com/MachineDawn_ai/status/2120543339728486897)).
- **Learn in public daily**: the "Day N/365 of robot learning" format ([@praneethsamine](https://x.com/praneethsamine/status/2120393376298070193)) and the overnight LLM-arm blog post that drew 81 points and 23 comments on HN ([blog](https://thisismypersonalblog.com/posts/2026-09-25-getting-out-of-the-way/), [HN summary](https://zeli.app/story/49879346)) are both cheap reach for a solo builder.
- **Open hardware can be a business**: Opulo grew from a home build to a 10-person open-source hardware company ([post](https://x.com/OpuloInc/status/2103551225186631846)).
- **Local talent**: Theker is in Barcelona, so the team can watch a well-funded modular-robot company from close by ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/theker-just-raised-85m-to-build-the-factory-robot-that-doesnt-specialize-in-anything/)).
