## H1. Collect-then-drive switching rule

- Title: Collect-then-drive switching rule for one mobile pusher.
- Source field: Animal herding, shepherding problem (collective-behaviour literature).
- Mechanism: One shepherd agent herds N agents that repel from it and attract to each other. At each step it checks group dispersion. If every agent lies within f(N) = r_a N^(2/3) of the group's centre of mass, it drives: it moves to a point directly behind the group on the line from the target through the centre, and the group's repulsion from it pushes the group toward the target. If any agent is further out than f(N), it collects: it moves to a point directly behind that furthest agent and pushes it back toward the group. It never moves within 3 r_a of any agent, because close approach splits the group into stable subgroups that the shepherd can no longer move. The herded agents need no knowledge of the target; the whole method runs off one dispersion measurement.
- Primary source: Strömbom D., Mann R.P., Wilson A.M., Hailes S., Morton A.J., Sumpter D.J.T., King A.J. (2014), "Solving the shepherding problem: heuristics for herding autonomous, interacting agents", Journal of the Royal Society Interface 11(100), 20140719, doi 10.1098/rsif.2014.0719. Fetched copy: https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa18314/Download/0018314-08072015111026.pdf. Quotes: "If at least one agent is further away than f(N) from the GCM" (the switch), "approach the flock at close range (since this causes the flock to fission)" (the standoff rule). The authors name the transfer themselves: "herd the movements of people to exits using a shepherd robot". Model code is catalogued at https://catalog.comses.net/publications/657.
- Transfer hypothesis: If riot squads had one mobile pusher (a water-spray UGV or a mounted pair) run on a collect-then-drive rule, switching from pushing the main body along the chosen route to closing behind the furthest splinter whenever that splinter passes a dispersion radius, then the number of breakaway groups free to loot side streets would change, measured by the count of groups above a set size outside the dispersion radius per minute of overwatch footage.
- Cheap test: Desk simulation with the published model (CoMSES code), one shepherd replaced by a UGV speed and standoff profile, agents given pedestrian speeds, compared with a straight line advancing at constant speed; output is splinter count over time. Two days, hardware-engineer. Then one interview question for customer-discovery: "On a riot night, how much of the damage comes from the main body and how much from breakaway groups, and what do you do about the breakaways?"
- Role to pick it up: hardware-engineer (engineering concept for UGV autonomy), with the interview question passed to customer-discovery.
- Prior art: Google Patents searches "herding robot shepherd livestock autonomous collecting driving flock patent" and "unmanned aerial vehicle herding livestock stray animal drive herd toward destination autonomous drone shepherd". Closest found: US20160295833A1 (granted as US10098324B2), "Herd Control Method and System", Jonathan O. Baize, priority 2015-04-09, https://patents.google.com/patent/US20160295833A1/en. It uses animal-worn devices that measure distance to peers and apply a stimulus to a strayed animal; it is not a mobile shepherd and has no drive-versus-collect switch. Nothing found on a mobile unit switching behaviour on group dispersion.
- Status: proposed.

## H2. Curved, solid-sided dispersal route

- Title: Curved, solid-sided dispersal route.
- Source field: Animal herding, cattle handling design.
- Mechanism: Cattle move more easily through a curved single-file race with solid sides. The curve stops them seeing people and the squeeze chute ahead until they are almost there, and it uses the animal's tendency to circle around the handler and to go back the way it came. Solid fences stop them seeing moving people, vehicles and other distractions outside, and wild cattle were observed to be more agitated in the part of a race where they could see out. The entrance must not bend sharply: an animal in the crowd pen must see at least two body lengths up the race or the entrance reads as a dead end and the animal balks. Grandin cites Vowles and Hollier (1982) for a reduction of up to 50% in time spent moving cattle with circular crowd pens and curved races, and notes that continuously moving stock showed no run-through difference between straight and curved races; the gain shows when animals wait in line.
- Primary source: Grandin T. (1997), "The design and construction of facilities for handling cattle", Livestock Production Science 49(2), 103-119. Author's copy fetched at https://grandin.com/references/design.construction.facilities.handling.cattle.html (journal reference confirmed at https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/10752655). Quotes: "a curved race prevents the animals from seeing people up ahead", "make the entrance to the race appear to be a dead end".
- Transfer hypothesis: If riot squads had opaque, curved barrier modules to line the designated exit street (the crowd cannot see the police line or vehicles at the far end, and the first bend starts well past the mouth so the route does not read as a dead end), then the rate at which a crowd takes the offered exit would change, measured by persons per metre width per minute entering the route and the number of stop-and-turn events at the mouth, first in simulation, then on overwatch footage.
- Cheap test: Tabletop with two or three officers over a street map and card modules (opaque versus mesh, straight versus curved), asking where crowds have refused an open street and what they could see at the time. One session, customer-discovery. If the balking problem is confirmed, hardware-engineer sketches a module that folds from a barrier vehicle and runs a desk simulation of the mouth geometry.
- Role to pick it up: customer-discovery (discovery question), then hardware-engineer.
- Prior art: Google Patents searches "mobile crowd control barrier vehicle deployable opaque panels corridor riot" and "portable solid panel barrier blocks line of sight crowd control curved corridor guiding pedestrians police". Closest found: WO2016082825A1, "Portable barrier for a crowd containment device", HMR Handels GmbH, priority 2014-11-28, https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2016082825A1/en: an open-grid fence on a plate base, chained in straight lines, with a closed-plate variant mentioned; no corridor or curve. Also US5611641A "Crowd control barrier system", https://patents.google.com/patent/US5611641. Nothing found on opaque curved modular dispersal corridors.
- Status: proposed.

## H3. Crowd pressure alarm on the overwatch drone

- Title: Crowd pressure alarm on the overwatch drone.
- Source field: Crowd science, Hajj crowd management after the Mina disasters.
- Mechanism: From video of the 12 January 2006 Mina crush, the flow went from laminar to stop-and-go waves, which started when outflow from the recorded area dropped below 0.8 persons per metre per second, then at higher density to irregular motion the authors call turbulent, where people are pushed in all directions and fall. Above a local density of 6 persons per square metre the local flow fell by a factor of 3. The authors define a crowd "pressure" as local density times local velocity variance; the accident began when this pressure peaked, about 10 minutes after turbulent motion set in and more than 30 minutes after average flow dropped below the critical value, with pressure above 0.02 per second squared. They state these warning signs can be computed online by automated video analysis. The 2007 Hajj measures (one-way operation, scheduling and routing, elliptical Jamarah shape) followed from this analysis.
- Primary source: Helbing D., Johansson A., Al-Abideen H.Z. (2007), "The Dynamics of Crowd Disasters: An Empirical Study", Physical Review E 75, 046109. Fetched at https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0701203 (journal reference confirmed at https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/arXiv:physics%2F0701203). Quotes: "the local pedestrian density times the local velocity variance", "The crowd accident began when the "pressure" reached its peak", "can be evaluated on-line by an automated video analysis system".
- Transfer hypothesis: If riot squads had a tethered overwatch drone that computes local density and velocity variance (crowd pressure) over the crowd front and at the mouth of the exit street, then the decision to halt an advance would follow the pressure trend instead of the commander's eye, measured by the lead time between the pressure alarm and the first fall or crush call in the radio log, and by the number of halts ordered per operation.
- Cheap test: Run an open-source pedestrian tracker on existing overhead footage of a dense French dispersal, or on the Love Parade 2010 footage already studied in the literature, and compute the pressure field with the paper's formula; three days, hardware-engineer. One interview question for customer-discovery: "How do you decide today that an advance must stop?" One check for regulation-procurement: density and velocity estimation on overhead footage without identification is not biometric identification under the AI Act; confirm.
- Role to pick it up: hardware-engineer (engineering concept, part of the overwatch drone product).
- Prior art: Google Patents search "crowd monitoring video density velocity early warning stampede system". Found: US10133937B2, "Crowd monitoring system", Hitachi Kokusai Electric, priority 2014-12-24, https://patents.google.com/patent/US10133937B2/en: video density estimation with a preset density threshold for a warning, no velocity variance and no pressure quantity. Also US20140372348A1, "Real-time anomaly detection of crowd behavior using multi-sensor information", https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140372348A1/en. The pressure metric itself is published prior art from 2007; what is new here is its use to pace a police advance.
- Status: proposed.

## H4. Advance pace governed by exit outflow (faster is slower)

- Title: Advance pace governed by exit outflow; halt when the narrowing clogs.
- Source field: Crowd science and fluids and traffic (pedestrian dynamics treated as granular flow). Two sweeps filed this mechanism independently; merged into one entry.
- Mechanism: In the Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek model, pedestrians leaving a room through an opening form arches at it once their desired velocity rises; friction between pressed bodies becomes dominant and the outflow drops. Above a desired velocity of about 1.5 metres per second leaving becomes less efficient, so pushing harder gives a lower average rate of leaving and higher pressure in the jam, up to 4,450 newtons per metre in the model; injured people become fixed obstacles. Pastor, Garcimartín and co-authors reproduced faster-is-slower experimentally in humans evacuating a room, in sheep entering a barn and in grains leaving a hopper. The column placed asymmetrically before the exit, proposed in the same paper, did not hold up in evacuation drills with up to 180 people, so only the pace effect transfers. In a dispersal the police advance sets the crowd's desired velocity toward the exit street; the model says that pushing past the exit's capacity lowers the real outflow and raises pressure at the mouth.
- Primary source: Helbing D., Farkas I., Vicsek T. (2000), "Simulating dynamical features of escape panic", Nature 407, 487-490, doi 10.1038/35035023. Fetched preprint: https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0009448. Quotes: "Desired velocities higher than 1.5 m/s reduce the efficiency of leaving", "trying to move faster (i.e., increasing v0 i) can cause a smaller average speed of leaving". Experimental confirmation: Pastor J.M. et al. (2015), https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05110v1, quote: "the FIS effect indeed occurs in three different systems". Counter-evidence on the column only: Garcimartín A. et al. (2018), "Redefining the role of obstacles in pedestrian evacuation", New Journal of Physics 20, 123025, https://core.ac.uk/download/188602409.pdf, quote: "pedestrians flow rate is not necessarily altered by the presence of an obstacle".
- Transfer hypothesis: If riot squads had an advance pace set by the measured outflow of the exit street (slow or halt as soon as persons per metre width per second through the mouth starts to fall while density at the mouth rises), then the time to clear the square, the peak density at the mouth and the longest stoppage of exit flow would change, measured in a social-force simulation and then on overwatch footage.
- Cheap test: Desk simulation in JuPedSim or Vadere: a square with one exit street, a moving wall for the police line, a sweep of advance speeds, continuous push against halt-when-flow-drops; outputs are clearance time, peak density at the mouth and the longest stoppage in seconds. Two working days (estimate), hardware-engineer. Interview questions for customer-discovery: "Have you seen a crowd jam at the mouth of the street you were pushing it into, and what did you do?" and "When the crowd you are pushing stalls at a narrowing, what happens at the front?"
- Role to pick it up: customer-discovery (discovery question); if the jam is confirmed, hardware-engineer runs the simulation and joins the pace rule to H3's drone.
- Prior art: Google Patents searches "pedestrian flow rate control bottleneck crowd density sensor gate metering evacuation speed signal" and "obstacle pillar placed in front of exit door to increase pedestrian evacuation flow prevent clogging arching crowd". Closest found: WO2017105639A1, "Systems and methods to direct foot traffic", Intel, https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2017105639A1/en: cues on personal devices, no outflow measurement at a bottleneck, no pacing. Nothing found on pacing a police line to exit outflow.
- Status: proposed.

## H5. Metered release with downstream feedback
- Title: Ramp metering at the cordon release.
- Source field: freeway traffic operations (ramp metering).
- Mechanism: Ramp meters are traffic signals on freeway on-ramps that control how often vehicles join the mainline. Two effects follow. Entry demand is held near the capacity of the freeway, so mainline flow does not break down into stop-and-go; and platoons arriving from the ramp are broken into single vehicles that merge into gaps instead of forcing mainline drivers to brake. Release is one car per green, or two where ramp capacity is short. The FHWA reports lower crash rates and higher throughput after metering.
- Primary source: FHWA, "Ramp Metering: A Proven, Cost-Effective Operational Strategy, A Primer", FHWA-HOP-14-020, https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop14020/sec1.htm (release policy in sec3.htm). Quotes: "break up the platoons by controlling the rate at which vehicles enter"; "prevents traffic flow breakdowns".
- Transfer hypothesis: If riot squads had a cordon release gate whose rate is set by measured crowd density downstream (the ramp-meter principle, density read from the overwatch drone), then re-grouping of released persons would fall, measured by the number of groups of twenty or more within 200 m of the gate ten minutes after release.
- Cheap test: One question in the officer interview: "Who sets the release rate of a nasse, from what, and what happens 100 m downstream?" Then a desk simulation (JuPedSim) of three release rates against downstream density. Oscar; one working day (estimate).
- Role to pick it up: engineering concept, hardware-engineer: a portable metering signal or gate fed by the drone's density map (the venture already plans the drone).
- Prior art: Google Patents, terms "crowd flow control gate release rate controlled by downstream pedestrian density sensor passage metering" and "subway station passenger flow control gate opening rate adjusted according to platform crowd density detected downstream". Found: US20200081418A1, Cubic, "Adaptive gateline configuration", https://patents.google.com/patent/US20200081418A1/en: gate count and direction set from measured throughput and queue length, not a release rate set by downstream density. Also read: EP3446552B1 (Signify), crowd steering by lighting and sound, no gates.

## H6. Gel pre-treatment of arson targets
- Title: Gel pre-treatment of arson targets.
- Source field: wildland firefighting, exposure protection with water enhancers (gels).
- Mechanism: Water enhancers are thickened water, mixed from a concentrate and sprayed on fuels or on exposed surfaces ahead of a fire; the patented versions use a superabsorbent polymer. The thickened water adheres to vertical surfaces and stays where it is put; the layer slows heat transfer from the fire and keeps oxygen from the surface. Protection lasts while the gel is wet: once its water has evaporated it is no longer effective, and more water can be added to form a new layer. The USDA specification qualifies products by a radiant-panel test comparing time to ignition of treated and untreated siding, with toxicity and corrosion limits.
- Primary source: USDA Forest Service, Specification 5100-306a, "Specification for Water Enhancers (Gels) for Wildland Firefighting", 1 June 2007, https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090119031816/http:/www.fs.fed.us/rm/fire/wfcs/documents/306a.pdf. Quotes: "for exposure protection"; "when that water has evaporated, they are no longer effective". Mechanism also read in US5849210A (below). Quote: "forms a thermal barrier that slows the transfer of heat from a fire".
- Transfer hypothesis: If riot squads had a gel pre-treatment kit for bins and parked cars on a known hotspot block before an announced night, then the share of ignition attempts that become sustained fires would fall, measured by burnt bins and vehicles per block, treated against untreated blocks.
- Cheap test: Small physical mock-up on private ground with an extinguisher at hand: two identical bins with the same paper load, one coated with a commercial gel, same ignition at the same point, time to sustained flame on the bin body. Oscar and Mathieu; half a day plus the gel order (estimate). Interview question for officers and a municipal contact: "Are the blocks where cars burn known the day before, and who could spray them?"
- Role to pick it up: discovery question, customer-discovery. Flag for regulation-procurement afterwards: spraying private vehicles needs a legal basis, and run-off rules apply.
- Prior art: Google Patents, US5849210A, "Method of preventing combustion by applying an aqueous superabsorbent polymer composition" (filed 1995, granted 1998), https://patents.google.com/patent/US5849210. Products exist on this patent family (Barricade gel) and as USDA-type water enhancers.

## H8. Range-governed water jet

- Title: Range-governed water jet.
- Source field: Policing abroad, water-cannon doctrine: South Korea (Baek Nam-gi case) and the UK review of the German WaWe 9000.
- Mechanism: The Korean police water-cannon guideline caps direct spray at a water force of 3,000 rpm (15 bar) and requires care not to hit protesters above the chest. In the Baek Nam-gi case the Constitutional Court found that the direct jet was held on his head and upper chest for about 13 seconds, and that a broken joystick and a faulty pressure limiter made fine aiming and force control difficult. It held the spray violated the least-restrictive-means requirement and the right to life. The injury-relevant quantity is force at impact, which depends on pump pressure and on the distance to the person, and the machine controlled neither: the cap lived in a document, not in the hardware. The UK Home Secretary's 2015 refusal of the German WaWe 9000 cited the SACMILL finding of possible spinal fracture, concussion and eye injury, and the Stuttgart protester blinded by a similar model.
- Primary source: Constitutional Court of Korea, 2015Hun-Ma1149, decided 23 April 2020, official text at the national law portal: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//detcInfoP.do?detcSeq=157645. Quote: "물살세기 3,000rpm(15bar) 이하로 살수한다" (spray at a water force of 3,000 rpm, 15 bar, or less). Quote: "이 사건 직사살수행위는 침해의 최소성에 반한다" (this direct spray violates the least-restrictive-means requirement). ## H9. Injury-risk-by-range dossier

- Title: Injury-risk-by-range dossier.
- Source field: Policing abroad, US Joint Intermediate Force Capabilities Office (JIFCO) human-effects literature.
- Mechanism: JIFCO characterises each intermediate-force device by a Risk of Significant Injury computed for each weapon-and-range pair. Finite-element body models for eight segments (skull, face, neck, thorax, abdomen, pelvis, arm, leg) give the probability that a hit causes a serious injury; the overall figure is a weighted sum over segments and vulnerability regions, weighted by hit probability and vulnerable area, with range entering through impact velocity and dispersion. An independent Human Effects Review Board reviews the analyses. The effect is that the injury risk at a given range is a number attached to the device before fielding, and employment tactics are written from that number rather than from the manufacturer's leaflet.
- Primary source: Bhatt, Swallow, Teichman and Macheret, "Guidance for Calculation of Risk of Significant Injury from Blunt Impact Projectiles", IDA Document NS D-31854, October 2021, https://www.ida.org/-/media/feature/publications/g/gu/guidance-for-calculation-of-risk-of-significant-injury-from-blunt-impact-projectiles/d-31854.ashx. Quote: "Range affects both impact velocity (mechanical response) and weapon x,y dispersion." Quote: "skull, face, neck, thorax, abdomen, pelvis, arm, and leg". Supporting: JIFCO, Non-Lethal Human Effects fact sheet, https://jifco.defense.gov/Press-Room/Fact-Sheets/Article-View-Fact-sheets/Article/577996/non-lethal-human-effects-fact-sheet/, quote: "Human Effects Review Board independently reviews non-lethal human effects research and analyses."
- Transfer hypothesis: "If riot squads had every intermediate-force device, water jets and foam sprayers included, delivered with a risk-of-significant-injury table by range reviewed by a medical board independent of the vendor and of the police, then the engagement distances officers choose would change, measured by the share of engagements in a tabletop exercise that fall inside the table's low-risk band before and after the table is shown."
- Cheap test: Desk check, half a day, regulation-procurement: does any French text (code de la sécurité intérieure, the instruction on armes de force intermédiaire, DGPN or DGGN employment notes, SAELSI or UGAP tender files) require an injury-risk characterisation by range, and does France have a body equivalent to SACMILL or JIFCO's review board? One interview question: "what is the minimum distance for the LBD 40 and where does the number come from?" Then a one-hour tabletop with two officers and a printed table.
- Role to pick it up: regulation-procurement (regulation check); the interview question goes to customer-discovery.
- Prior art: the method is public. The IDA guidance above and the UK SACMILL statements (publication list: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/water-cannon-trial-reports-and-medical-implications) are the prior art. Google Patents search "method assessing injury risk non-lethal kinetic projectile impact safe distance less-lethal": only device-level prior art, Safariland US8756852B2 (above). ## H10. Two seals per block-step

- Title: Two seals per block-step.
- Source field: Nature and games, containment games on graphs (the Firefighter problem).
- Mechanism: A fire starts at one vertex. Each turn the defender protects one unburnt vertex, then the fire spreads to every unprotected neighbour. On the infinite square grid one firefighter never contains the fire; two are necessary and sufficient, and two contain it in eight turns with 18 vertices burnt (Moeller and Wang; Develin and Hartke). Three contain it in two turns with two vertices burnt. The result is about tempo: what counts is the number of sealings per spread step, not the total number of barriers owned. Below two per step on a grid the spread is unbounded; at two it is contained in a bounded area.
- Primary source: Finbow and MacGillivray, "The Firefighter Problem: a survey of results, directions and questions", Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 43 (2009), https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/43/ajc_v43_p057.pdf. Quote: "If n = 2 then two firefighters are necessary and sufficient to contain the fire." Quote: "a single firefighter can not contain the fire in an infinite 2-dimensional square grid."
- Transfer hypothesis: "If riot squads had barrier vehicles able to seal two intersections in the time a looting group takes to advance one block, then the spread of looting across a grid-plan district would change from unbounded to contained, measured by the number of intersections reached in a tabletop replay on a real street map with one, two and three sealings per block-step."
- Cheap test: Tabletop: a printed map of a grid-plan district, a spread rule (the group reaches every adjacent intersection each step, the worst case), a defender placing one, two or three seals per step; twenty replays per setting; two hours; hardware-engineer runs it, customer-discovery observes. ## H11. Escape waves point to the source

- Title: Escape waves point to the source.
- Source field: Nature and games, starling flock responses to a predator.
- Mechanism: When a falcon attacks a starling flock, a dark band travels through the flock: birds bank in turn, so the wave is a change of orientation, not of density. The band moves continuously from one end of the flock to the other, always away from the predator, at an average 13.4 m/s, close to the predator's own 11 to 15 m/s, and the model shows that local interactions with two to seven nearest neighbours suffice to produce that speed: no bird needs to see the falcon for the wave to run. For an outside observer the wave's origin and direction give the predator's position and heading even when the predator is too small to see.
- Primary source: Hemelrijk, van Zuidam and Hildenbrandt, "What underlies waves of agitation in starling flocks", Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology 69 (2015), https://core.ac.uk/download/518702747.pdf. Quote: "always away from the predator". Quote: "waves move on average at a speed of 13.4 m/s". Not fetched (blocked): Storms et al. 2019, "Complex patterns of collective escape in starling flocks under predation", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6404399.
- Transfer hypothesis: "If riot squads had the tethered overwatch drone's video processed for orientation waves, a front of people turning away from one point and running across the crowd, then the time to locate a flashpoint or the start of a breakaway would change, measured by the seconds between the first frame of the wave and the first radio report of the incident, on recorded footage."
- Cheap test: Desk: three public fixed-camera riot videos with a known incident (a charge, a thrown object, a fire), dense optical flow in OpenCV, detection of a propagating front of direction change, its origin marked and compared with the visible incident in place and time; hardware-engineer, two days. One interview question: "how do you learn where it started: radio, the drone operator, or seeing the crowd move?"
- Role to pick it up: hardware-engineer (engineering concept for the drone payload software); the interview question goes to customer-discovery.
- Prior art: Google Patents search "crowd abnormal behavior detection optical flow escape panic wave video surveillance". Found: Patriot One Technologies, US11935303B2, "System and method for mitigating crowd panic detection", https://patents.google.com/patent/US11935303: dense optical flow against a motion baseline, flags panic by block and quadrant, does not track a propagating front or locate its origin. Also listed, not fetched: CN106022234A, abnormal crowd behaviour detection based on optical flow, https://patents.google.com/patent/CN106022234A/en. Origin localisation from the wave is the gap.
