# Constraints for design (first pass, 2026-10-31; sources in legal-map.md)

1. Nothing the company makes may be a weapon unless it is on the exhaustive list of CSI R. 211-11, R. 211-16 and D. 211-17; water projection ("engins lanceurs d'eau") is on it, foam and unmanned carriers are unverified (Q2, Q3).
2. Any device that projects force must allow graduated, proportionate use (R. 211-13) and must be usable only after the second sommation is repeated (R. 211-11): a remote operator needs a confirm step tied to the sommations.
3. Aerial cameras, tethered included (L. 242-1 "aéronefs captifs"), may not capture sound and may not run facial recognition or any automated matching with other files (L. 242-4).
4. Aerial recording may not be permanent; images are deleted at most seven days after the deployment ends; images of the inside of homes are not to be captured (L. 242-4, L. 242-5): design storage to expire and to mask.
5. Every drone deployment runs under a préfet's written authorisation with a perimeter; the product must log where it flew, when, and under which authorisation (L. 242-5).
6. No real-time remote biometric identification function, ever (AI Act Article 5(1)(h)); no post identification function either unless built as a high-risk AI system under Article 26(10) and Annex III, which is a different company.
7. A street-sealing barrier must leave a controlled exit for people (CE n° 444849 on encirclement, para 22) and be removable fast enough to be proportionate.
8. If a vehicle is armoured or carries a listed weapon, check war-materiel classification (CSI R. 311-2, code de la défense) before any drawing (Q1).
9. Fire-suppression devices are sécurité civile equipment: design to SDIS and BSPP needs, not police needs, and to their buying cadence.
10. Every logged action should be exportable in a form a judge can read: the Conseil d'État reviews these operations under faute lourde and proportionality.
