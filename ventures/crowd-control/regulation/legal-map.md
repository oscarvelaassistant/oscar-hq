Encirclement without a stated doctrine was annulled by the Conseil d'État (point 3.1.4 of the 2020 SNMO), which matters for barrier vehicles used to close streets.

## The AI Act position on after-the-fact footage review

- Prohibition, verbatim from Article 5(1)(h) (Commission AI Act service desk page, consolidated text as at 27 July 2026): "the use of 'real-time' remote biometric identification systems in publicly accessible spaces" "for the purposes of law enforcement, unless and in so far as such use is strictly necessary for one of the following objectives", with exceptions (i) "the targeted search for specific victims of abduction, trafficking in human beings or sexual exploitation" and missing persons, (ii) "the prevention of a specific, substantial and imminent threat to the life or physical safety of natural persons" or a terrorist attack, (iii) localisation or identification of a suspect for Annex II offences punishable by at least four years. Article 5(2) and 5(3): per-use prior authorisation by a judicial or independent administrative authority, fundamental rights impact assessment, registration, 24-hour rule in urgency.
- After-the-fact (post) remote biometric identification is not in Article 5. It is a high-risk use (Annex III point 1(a), not fetched) with deployer duties in Article 26(10), read 2026-10-31 on the Commission service desk page (consolidated "as at 27 July 2026"): the deployer "shall request an authorisation, ex ante" or "no later than 48 hours" after use, from a judicial or binding administrative authority, "in the framework of an investigation for the targeted search of a person"; a refused authorisation stops the use and deletes the data; no untargeted use; "no decision that produces an adverse legal effect on a person may be taken" solely on the output; each use documented in the police file; "Deployers shall submit annual reports to the relevant market surveillance" and data protection authorities; Member States may be stricter. The page carries no "NEW" or "AMENDED" marker on Article 26.
- Dates, Article 113 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the "Digital Omnibus on AI"), read 2026-10-31 on euaiact.com, a secondary reproduction that says EUR-Lex governs: general application "2 August 2026"; Chapters I and II (prohibitions included) from "2 February 2025", except Article 5(1) first subparagraph points (ba) and (bb) and Article 5(1a) and (1b), which apply from "2 December 2026"; Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII, Chapter XII and Article 78 from "2 August 2025"; Chapter III Sections 1 to 3 from 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk systems (post remote biometric identification sits there) and from 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems; Articles 102 to 110 from 27 July 2026. So the "two additional prohibitions" of the service desk summary are the new points (ba) and (bb) of Article 5(1); their content was not read this run (Q4, narrowed). The EUR-Lex consolidated text remains to be fetched; the 2024 Official Journal text at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj predates the amendment.
- The two added prohibitions, read 2026-11-01 on the Commission Article 5 page (marked NEW): point (ba) covers AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate images, video or audio of an identifiable person without consent; point (bb) covers material within Directive 2011/93/EU (child sexual abuse); paragraphs (1a) and (1b) narrow them to systems intended for that purpose. Neither touches crowd control. Q4 closed.
- So the idea note's sentence "the EU AI Act limits real-time facial recognition, so footage is reviewed afterwards" is half right: the prohibition is on real-time use; the "afterwards" path is itself regulated as high-risk under EU law and, for drone footage, closed by French law (no facial recognition on aerial cameras, seven-day retention).

## Use of force and weapons, French texts (as verified this run)
- CSI L. 211-9: an attroupement "dissipé par la force publique, après deux sommations de se disperser"; direct use of force when violence is used against the forces or they cannot otherwise hold their ground (ministry answer to QE 16844, 24 Sep 2019).
- CSI R. 211-11 last paragraph: the second sommation is repeated if weapons are to be used (same source).
- CSI R. 211-16 and D. 211-17 with R. 211-11: the exhaustive list of permitted weapons; intermediate means named in the answer: bâtons de défense, engins lanceurs d'eau, hand-thrown tear gas grenades (MP7, CM6); LBD excluded from dispersal after the second sommation (same source).
- CSI R. 211-13: "La force déployée doit être proportionnée au trouble à faire cesser" (CE n° 468316, 31 May 2024).
- CSI L. 435-1: use of weapons "en cas d'absolue nécessité et de manière strictement proportionnée" (same decision).
- D. 211-17 classifies instantaneous tear gas grenades (MP7) as category A2 weapons (same decision). Relevance: anything the company makes that is a weapon needs a classification check under R. 311-2 before design (Q1, Q2).
- SNMO: the 16 September 2020 scheme was partly annulled by CE 10 June 2021 n° 444849, re-read 2026-10-31. Dispositif (article 3): in point 2.2.1 the words "dès lors que leur identification est confirmée" and the rest of that sentence; in point 2.2.2 the words "accrédités auprès des autorités"; point 2.2.4 in full (the dispersal offence applied to journalists, considérants 20 and 21: the criminal provisions cannot by themselves keep the press away, journalists and independent observers may keep working during a dispersal if they are not confused with demonstrators and do not hinder the forces); point 3.1.4 in full (encirclement: considérant 22 restates the point, which said "il peut être utile" to encircle for a strictly necessary time and that "il est systématiquement laissé un point de sortie contrôlé"; considérant 28 holds that the technique "peut s'avérer nécessaire" but "est susceptible d'affecter significativement la liberté de manifester" and that the point did not frame its cases of use precisely enough). ## Weapons classification (Q1, Q2), Légifrance read 2026-11-01
- CSI R. 311-1 (version in force since 7 September 2025, décret 2025-894): "arme" is "tout objet ou dispositif conçu ou destiné par nature à tuer, blesser" (definition 2°, opening words); part IV excludes objects under 2 joules at the muzzle and some rescue and industrial tools.
- CSI R. 311-2 (same version): four categories A to D; A2 is "Les armes relevant des matériels de guerre" and lists, among others, "lanceurs à munition intégrée" (4°), "Bombes, torpilles, mines, missiles, grenades, engins incendiaires" (6°) and "Véhicules de combat blindés ou non blindés" (8°). What it means: a barrier vehicle is war materiel only if it is a "véhicule de combat"; whether an armoured street-sealing vehicle with no weapon is one is the question for the classification desk or a lawyer (Q1). A water projector is not on the A2 list read; its status follows R. 211-16 and D. 211-17 (Q2, Q3).

### Q3 closed: D. 211-17 lists grenades and launchers only; no water, no foam

- R. 211-16 (in force since 1 December 2014, décret 2014-1253 art. 9): outside the two cases of L. 211-9 sixth paragraph, the firearms that may be used to maintain public order are grenades "principalement à effet de souffle" and their launchers, "autorisés par décret". Same page.

"Engins lanceurs d'eau" are not in it. The ministry's 2019 answer named water cannon among intermediate means, not as a D. 211-17 item. So a water or foam projector's legality does not rest on D. 211-17: it rests on the general rules (L. 211-9 sommations, R. 211-13 proportionality, L. 435-1 for weapons) and on the SNMO's doctrine, and the live question is Q12: is a remote projector an "arme" under R. 311-1 at all, and if so in which R. 311-2 category. Foam is in exactly the same position as water: neither is named anywhere in the section.

### Q7 closed: what décision 2021-834 DC (20 January 2022) did to L. 242-1 to L. 242-8
- Article 1 of the decision declares contrary to the Constitution, in article 15 of the law: the words "et L. 242-7" in the second alinéa of 2° and in a of 5°; the twenty-fifth alinéa of 6° (the urgency procedure that let State services fly cameras "pour une durée pouvant atteindre quatre heures" after merely informing the préfet, §31); and 8° (the police municipale experiment, §§34 to 39: purpose not limited to events exposed to serious disorder, no power for the préfet to end an authorisation, same urgency defect).
- Article 2 declares conform, with reserves: L. 242-4 first two sentences of the first alinéa and the second alinéa (reserves §27 and §30); L. 242-5 I to IV, VI and VII (reserve §28). The three reserves: §27, the préfet may authorise only after finding that the service "ne peut employer d'autres moyens moins intrusifs au regard de ce droit", and the authorisation fixes "le périmètre strictement nécessaire" and "le nombre maximal de caméras pouvant être utilisées simultanément"; §28, renewal only if "le recours à ces dispositifs aéroportés demeure le seul moyen d'atteindre la finalité poursuivie"; §30, the ban on facial recognition on the device cannot be read as allowing analysis of the images by other automated facial recognition systems.
- §29: the devices must "ne recueillir ni les images de l'intérieur des domiciles ni, de façon spécifique, celles de leurs entrées"; where capture happens "l'enregistrement doit être immédiatement interrompu"; where it could not be, "les images enregistrées sont supprimées dans un délai qui ne peut excéder quarante-huit heures" from the end of the deployment, unless transmitted to the judicial authority. This 48-hour rule concerns home images only; the decision contains no general retention period for drone images (its only "sept jours" is §11, custody cameras).
- The dispositif does not name L. 242-1, L. 242-2, L. 242-8 or L. 242-5 V. The seven-day retention of L. 242-4 therefore stands as promulgated; the article's text in force on Légifrance was not read this run (article pages refused; doctrine.fr blocked robots), so that sentence still rests on the CNIL annex and the consolidation PDF.
- Consequences for design: a drone product's authorisation log should record the perimeter and the maximum number of cameras authorised (§27), and storage needs two clocks: 48 hours for any sequence flagged as showing a home or its entrance, seven days for the rest.

### Q8 answered on encirclement, open on equipment: the SNMO of December 2021
- The text is a circular of 15 December 2021, NOR INTK2137104J, signed by the minister, online 22 December 2021 (Légifrance circulaire page, read 2026-11-01). Its PDF (18.8 MB) was not reached this run.
- What it says on encirclement, as quoted by the Conseil d'État when it reviewed that version (CE 29 December 2023, n° 461513 and 461598, read 2026-11-01): point 3.1.4 allows encirclement to "prévenir ou faire cesser des violences graves et imminentes contre les personnes et les biens", for "une durée strictement nécessaire et proportionnée", and requires to "systématiquement ménager un point de sortie contrôlé" (considérants 9 and 13; the exit may be withheld only under particular public-order constraints). The Conseil d'État rejected the challenge to 3.1.4 (considérant 15) and held that the controlled exit allows no identity check outside CPP article 78-2 (considérant 14). It annulled point 2.2.3.3 in so far as it excludes independent observers from staying during a dispersal (considérants 6 and 7, dispositif article 1). Journalists may "circuler librement" (2.2.3.2, upheld) and "peuvent porter des équipements de protection" (2.2.3.4, upheld).
- On equipment the Conseil d'État decision says nothing (no LBD, grenade, water or drone passage). Secondary sources, flagged as such until the PDF is read: the December 2021 version replaces the GLI-F4 with the GM2L and the GMD with "un modèle plus récent moins vulnérant", places a supervisor with LBD shooters, and extends the equipment and training obligation to units beyond the EGM and CRS (faceaurisque.com summary); the Observatoire parisien des libertés publiques communiqué of 23 December 2021 quotes the encirclement wording and the exit "dès que les circonstances de l'ordre public le permettent".
- What it means: the barrier direction's doctrine condition is now precise: a street closure used to encircle needs a controlled exit by default, a stated grave-and-imminent-violence case, and a duration the commander can justify. A barrier product should make the exit a built-in feature, not an afterthought.

### Q11 closed: L. 2172-3 wording and the R. 2123-1 b condition
- L. 2172-3 (reproduction on code-commande-publique.com, page last modified 6 May 2025; Légifrance article page refused twice): first paragraph defines the partenariat d'innovation ("la recherche et le développement de produits, services ou travaux innovants ainsi que l'acquisition ultérieure" of the result, for a need "ne pouvant être satisfait par l'acquisition de produits, services ou travaux déjà disponibles sur le marché"); second paragraph: "Sont considérés comme innovants les travaux, fournitures ou services nouveaux ou sensiblement améliorés", including new production, marketing or organisational methods. The young-enterprises sentence added by loi n° 2023-1322 du 29 décembre 2023 (art. 44) was removed by loi n° 2025-391 du 30 avril 2025 (art. 15), per the same reproduction; the removal rests on that one source this run.
- R. 2123-1, 2° (reproduction on marche-public.fr, "Modifié par Décret n° 2021-357 du 30 mars 2021"): a) each lot under "80 000 euros hors taxes pour des fournitures ou des services" or 1 million EUR for works; b) "Le montant cumulé de ces lots n'excède pas 20 % de la valeur totale estimée de tous les lots".
- What it means: the under-100,000 EUR innovation purchase (R. 2122-9-1) is open to any supplier whose product is "nouveau ou sensiblement amélioré"; being a young company no longer counts by itself. Inside a larger contract, innovation lots bought this way may not exceed 20 percent of the whole.
