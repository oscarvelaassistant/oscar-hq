| 4. Evidence capture for later prosecution, footage reviewed afterwards | Needs a change to the idea note's assumption. Two layers: (a) under French law, drone footage may be kept at most seven days (L. 242-4) and the device may carry no automated facial recognition and no interconnection with other files, so "review afterwards" means human review within seven days, not a biometric search; (b) under the AI Act, real-time remote biometric identification for law enforcement in public spaces is prohibited with three narrow exceptions (Article 5(1)(h), applicable since 2 February 2025 per Article 113 as read on 2026-10-31 in a secondary reproduction; EUR-Lex not yet fetched), while post (non real-time) remote biometric identification is a high-risk use with its own authorisation regime (Article 26(10), read on the Commission service desk page 2026-10-31; Annex III point 1 not fetched). Verdict: footage capture and human review is legal; any automated identification, even after the fact, is a regulated high-risk system under EU law and prohibited on aerial cameras under French law. | Article 5(1)(h) AI Act (fetched); CSI L. 242-4 |
| 5. Small unmanned vehicles that spray water or foam | Water projection is an existing maintien de l'ordre means: "engins lanceurs d'eau" are listed among intermediate means in the ministry's own answer, within the exhaustive list fixed by CSI R. 211-11, R. 211-16 and D. 211-17 and after the sommations of L. 211-9. A new water or foam device is legal if it is used by the forces under those articles; the open questions are whether an unmanned carrier changes the weapon classification (Q2) and whether foam counts as a listed means (Q3). | AN written question 16844 (answer of 24 Sep 2019); CSI L. 211-9, R. 211-11, R. 211-13, R. 211-16, D. 211-17 |

Common to all five: force must be "proportionnée au trouble à faire cesser" (R. 211-13, quoted by CE 31 May 2024 n° 468316) and weapons may only be used after the second sommation is repeated (R. 211-11 last paragraph, per the ministry's answer). Encirclement without a stated doctrine was annulled by the Conseil d'État (point 3.1.4 of the 2020 SNMO), which matters for barrier vehicles used to close streets.

## The AI Act position on after-the-fact footage review

- Prohibition, verbatim from Article 5(1)(h) (Commission AI Act service desk page, consolidated text as at 27 July 2026): "the use of 'real-time' remote biometric identification systems in publicly accessible spaces" "for the purposes of law enforcement, unless and in so far as such use is strictly necessary for one of the following objectives", with exceptions (i) "the targeted search for specific victims of abduction, trafficking in human beings or sexual exploitation" and missing persons, (ii) "the prevention of a specific, substantial and imminent threat to the life or physical safety of natural persons" or a terrorist attack, (iii) localisation or identification of a suspect for Annex II offences punishable by at least four years. Article 5(2) and 5(3): per-use prior authorisation by a judicial or independent administrative authority, fundamental rights impact assessment, registration, 24-hour rule in urgency.
- After-the-fact (post) remote biometric identification is not in Article 5. It is a high-risk use (Annex III point 1(a), not fetched) with deployer duties in Article 26(10), read 2026-10-31 on the Commission service desk page (consolidated "as at 27 July 2026"): the deployer "shall request an authorisation, ex ante" or "no later than 48 hours" after use, from a judicial or binding administrative authority, "in the framework of an investigation for the targeted search of a person"; a refused authorisation stops the use and deletes the data; no untargeted use; "no decision that produces an adverse legal effect on a person may be taken" solely on the output; each use documented in the police file; "Deployers shall submit annual reports to the relevant market surveillance" and data protection authorities; Member States may be stricter. The page carries no "NEW" or "AMENDED" marker on Article 26.
- Dates, Article 113 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the "Digital Omnibus on AI"), read 2026-10-31 on euaiact.com, a secondary reproduction that says EUR-Lex governs: general application "2 August 2026"; Chapters I and II (prohibitions included) from "2 February 2025", except Article 5(1) first subparagraph points (ba) and (bb) and Article 5(1a) and (1b), which apply from "2 December 2026"; Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII, Chapter XII and Article 78 from "2 August 2025"; Chapter III Sections 1 to 3 from 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk systems (post remote biometric identification sits there) and from 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems; Articles 102 to 110 from 27 July 2026. So the "two additional prohibitions" of the service desk summary are the new points (ba) and (bb) of Article 5(1); their content was not read this run (Q4, narrowed). The EUR-Lex consolidated text remains to be fetched; the 2024 Official Journal text at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj predates the amendment.
- So the idea note's sentence "the EU AI Act limits real-time facial recognition, so footage is reviewed afterwards" is half right: the prohibition is on real-time use; the "afterwards" path is itself regulated as high-risk under EU law and, for drone footage, closed by French law (no facial recognition on aerial cameras, seven-day retention).

## Use of force and weapons, French texts (as verified this run)
- CSI L. 211-9: an attroupement "dissipé par la force publique, après deux sommations de se disperser"; direct use of force when violence is used against the forces or they cannot otherwise hold their ground (ministry answer to QE 16844, 24 Sep 2019).
- CSI R. 211-11 last paragraph: the second sommation is repeated if weapons are to be used (same source).
- CSI R. 211-16 and D. 211-17 with R. 211-11: the exhaustive list of permitted weapons; intermediate means named in the answer: bâtons de défense, engins lanceurs d'eau, hand-thrown tear gas grenades (MP7, CM6); LBD excluded from dispersal after the second sommation (same source).
- CSI R. 211-13: "La force déployée doit être proportionnée au trouble à faire cesser" (CE n° 468316, 31 May 2024).
- CSI L. 435-1: use of weapons "en cas d'absolue nécessité et de manière strictement proportionnée" (same decision).
- D. 211-17 classifies instantaneous tear gas grenades (MP7) as category A2 weapons (same decision). Relevance: anything the company makes that is a weapon needs a classification check under R. 311-2 before design (Q1, Q2).
- SNMO: the 16 September 2020 scheme was partly annulled by CE 10 June 2021 n° 444849, re-read 2026-10-31. Dispositif (article 3): in point 2.2.1 the words "dès lors que leur identification est confirmée" and the rest of that sentence; in point 2.2.2 the words "accrédités auprès des autorités"; point 2.2.4 in full (the dispersal offence applied to journalists, considérants 20 and 21: the criminal provisions cannot by themselves keep the press away, journalists and independent observers may keep working during a dispersal if they are not confused with demonstrators and do not hinder the forces); point 3.1.4 in full (encirclement: considérant 22 restates the point, which said "il peut être utile" to encircle for a strictly necessary time and that "il est systématiquement laissé un point de sortie contrôlé"; considérant 28 holds that the technique "peut s'avérer nécessaire" but "est susceptible d'affecter significativement la liberté de manifester" and that the point did not frame its cases of use precisely enough). The word "nasse" does not appear in the decision. A revised SNMO was issued in December 2021 (not fetched).

## Aerial cameras (drones, tethered aircraft), French texts
- Legal basis: CSI L. 242-1 to L. 242-8, created by the loi relative à la responsabilité pénale et à la sécurité intérieure, reviewed by the Conseil constitutionnel in décision 2021-834 DC of 20 January 2022; regulatory texts R. 242-8 to R. 242-14 for police and gendarmerie, R. 242-1 to R. 242-7 and décret 2022-712 of 27 April 2022 for sécurité civile (CNIL annex 4, June 2025).
- L. 242-1: devices "opérés par un télépilote ou sur des aéronefs captifs" (consolidated text).
- L. 242-4: "ne peuvent ni procéder à la captation du son" "ni comporter de traitements automatisés de reconnaissance faciale"; "aucun rapprochement, interconnexion ou mise en relation automatisé"; "Elle ne peut être permanente"; retention "pendant une durée maximale de sept jours à compter de la fin du déploiement".
- L. 242-5: police and gendarmerie purposes include "La sécurité des rassemblements de personnes sur la voie publique" "lorsque ces rassemblements sont susceptibles d'entraîner des troubles graves à l'ordre public"; "L'autorisation est délivrée par décision écrite et motivée du représentant de l'État dans le département"; "pour une durée maximale de trois mois, renouvelable" and, for a gathering, for its duration only; perimeter "ne peut excéder le périmètre géographique strictement nécessaire"; no images of the inside of homes.
- What the Conseil constitutionnel decided on these articles: not read this run (the consolidation PDF predates the decision). Verify.
