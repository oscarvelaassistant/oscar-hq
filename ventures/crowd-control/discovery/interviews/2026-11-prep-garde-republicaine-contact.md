# Interview prep: former gendarme, Garde républicaine, motorbike escort (friend of Oscar's father)

Conversation planned: next week (date, name and place to fill). Face to face. One hour. Oscar alone, notebook, no laptop.

## Who he is for the venture (read before deciding what to ask)
Three things, in decreasing certainty:
1. An insider on the gendarmerie as an institution: how units are organised, how a sous-officier sees orders, equipment and risk, how outsiders are received. Certain.
2. A door: he knows people who stood riot nights (gendarmerie mobile squadrons, and through them CRS and BAC colleagues), and probably people on the equipment side. Likely.
3. A witness, possibly: Garde républicaine motorcyclists move officials through Paris; large demonstrations and the 2018 to 2019 and 2023 disorders put escorts and institutional guards in contact with hostile crowds and blocked, burning streets. Ask, do not assume. If he never stood one, say so in the notes and use him for 1 and 2.

The venture's first objective is a conversation with someone who has stood a riot night. He may be that person; if not, the win of this conversation is two or three names and the right way to approach them.

## What we are looking for, and why (each line is a decision it feeds)
| We want to learn | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which units are actually overwhelmed on riot nights, at which moments (first hour, after midnight, dispersal, holding a crossroads, escorting a convoy) | Picks the customer. "French riot police" is five different buyers: CRS, gendarmerie mobile, BAC, BRAV-M, municipal police. The product follows the unit. |
| What breaks down: mobility (blocked streets, burning obstacles, vehicles that cannot pass or get trapped), fire (cars, bins, no firefighters until the zone is secured), communications, fatigue and rotation, water and food, legal hesitation (when to engage), intelligence on the crowd (where the breakaway groups go) | Tells us which product direction is a real pain and which is an engineer's fantasy. Barrier vehicles, fire-suppression robots, tethered drones, evidence capture and water or foam UGVs each map to one line here. |
| What they improvised, and what they would never want again | Improvisations are unpaid prototypes; "never again" is a list of things not to build. |
| What was tried and shelved, and why (vehicles, drones, cannons, new gear that arrived and was not used) | Avoids repeating a known failure; the reason it failed is often doctrine or law, not technology. |
| How equipment reaches a squadron: who decides, who tests, who can say no, how long it takes, what a small company can realistically sell first | Picks the entry product and the first buyer; procurement politics is one of Oscar's three named risks. |
| How a young reserve sergeant with a civilian job gets taken seriously by serving officers | Shapes every later conversation. |
| Two or three names, and permission to say he sent us | Objective 1 if he is not himself a riot-night witness; the pipeline either way. |

## Questions, in order (French; keep the order, stories before opinions)
Warm-up (5 min)
1. Racontez-moi votre parcours : comment on arrive à la Garde républicaine, et à l'escorte moto ?
2. Qu'est-ce qui, dans ce métier, ne se voit pas de l'extérieur ?

His own hardest nights (20 min, this is the core)
3. Est-ce que vous avez connu des situations de foule hostile, en escorte ou ailleurs ? Racontez-moi la plus marquante, du début à la fin : l'heure, l'endroit, ce que vous voyiez, ce qu'on vous demandait de faire.
4. À ce moment-là, qu'est-ce qui vous manquait ? Matériel, information, ordres, monde ?
5. Qu'est-ce que vous avez bricolé ou improvisé pour vous en sortir ?
6. Qu'est-ce que vous ne voudriez plus jamais revivre ?
7. En moto, dans une ville en désordre : rues bloquées, feux, projectiles, comment on passe ? Qu'est-ce qui arrête un convoi ?

The institution (15 min)
8. Dans votre expérience, qui est vraiment en sous-nombre les nuits d'émeute, et à quel moment de la nuit ? Les escadrons mobiles, les CRS, la BAC, les motards ?
9. Comment un nouveau matériel arrive-t-il jusqu'à une unité ? Qui l'essaie, qui décide, qui peut dire non, combien de temps ça prend ?
10. Vous avez vu arriver des équipements qui n'ont jamais servi ? Pourquoi ?
11. Les lanceurs d'eau, les drones, les barrières : qu'est-ce qui marche, qu'est-ce qui est pour la photo ?

Reactions to directions (10 min, only now, and framed as other people's ideas)
12. Des gens proposent des choses comme : des véhicules qui bouclent une rue en quelques minutes, des robots ou drones qui éteignent les voitures et les poubelles en feu, des drones captifs qui donnent une carte de la foule en direct, des petits engins qui projettent de l'eau ou de la mousse. Lesquelles vous font hausser les épaules, lesquelles vous auraient servi ?
13. Qu'est-ce qui rendrait un industriel crédible à vos yeux, ou au contraire ridicule ?

The asks (10 min)
14. Qui, dans vos connaissances, a tenu des nuits comme celles de 2005, des Gilets jaunes ou de l'été 2023, et accepterait un café avec moi ? Est-ce que je peux dire que vous m'envoyez ?
15. Qui connaît le côté matériel et achats, côté gendarmerie ?
16. Si vous étiez à ma place, par quoi commenceriez-vous ?

## What to bring and what to leave at home
- Bring: a notebook, the sixteen questions on one page, his message thread, the names of the three or four product directions in your head, not on paper.
- Leave: slides, the word "startup", the word "IA", any drawing. He is the expert in the room; you are there to listen.
- Do not record. Ask at the end if you may quote him to the people he introduces.
- If he tells a story, let it run. The schedule above bends; question 3 matters more than 8 to 13 together.
