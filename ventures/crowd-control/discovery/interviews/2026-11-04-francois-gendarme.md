- **Date:** Wed 4 Nov 2026, 18:00 Europe/Madrid

- **Format:** Call
- **Source:** Oscar's notes sent 9 Nov 2026 ~10:21 Europe/Madrid

## Oscar's summary

Ended up having a really interesting talk with him. Long story short, he felt that the biggest issue that police faced is to be able to have proof of who did what. For example, if everyone is dressed in all black, wearing masks, and generally not identifiable, it makes it really hard to actually identify who did what, and even if you catch the guy, the security cameras just cannot determine who is who. So he basically said that one of the things that would help the most, and that there is just no solution for right now, is how you can differentiate the guy who threw a Molotov cocktail from the guy who broke the window from the guy who was just around there looking. He also said that there must be a way to use their phones against them, such as getting their IP address or something like that. And he said that social media is actually really big for people to organize the looting. So if there was a way to block or jam social media in a street or an area, that would be a big help.

## Key signals

1. The main pain is individualising responsibility in a masked crowd (Molotov thrower vs window breaker vs onlooker). There is no current solution. Cameras alone cannot tell who is who when everyone is in black and masked.
2. Constraints he named for any solution: legal, strictly non-lethal, saves money, no extra payroll (more capacity without raising the wage bill).
3. Ideas he raised: marking people for later identification (chemical marker or a traceable taser), using phones against them (device ID, IP), temporary local social-media blocking or jamming in a perimeter and time window, a taser drone or a phone-jamming drone.
4. Context he named: no patrol presence (always reactive, chasing after the thief), financial pressure with little solution, legal framework that blocks hard force, the Swiss mass-kettle-and-ID approach (surround everyone, feed them, take fingerprints and national ID cards all day), and the state shifting defence onto shopkeepers (which he sees as the state rid of its responsibility, feeding extreme politics and higher cost of life as insecurity rises). Soft materials preferred in France because striking a person gets the gendarme in trouble; obsession with physical integrity of individuals.

## What it changes

The problem may be identification and evidence after the fact, not control on the line. Soft material means that let units find people later (marking, phone-side traces) fit his framing better than tools that only contain a crowd.
