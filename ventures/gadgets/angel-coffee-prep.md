## What the coffee is for
A discovery meeting that earns a second one. Not a raise. The Holloway guide's framing of a first investor meeting matches: it is for getting to know each other and learning what the investor cares about; the end goal is their saying they want to meet again; the deck stays closed and serves at most for one chart ([Holloway, The First Meeting](https://holloway.com/g/venture-capital/sections/the-first-meeting)).

## Walk out with (three things)
1. Her investor profile: has she invested before, in what, at what ticket, and what she wants from it (returns, a project in Europe, a bridge for her Shenzhen contacts). If she has never invested, she is a Shenzhen connection, which is still valuable; treat her as that.
2. One introduction offered, not asked twice: a Shenzhen ODM, sourcing agent or factory contact who could build a sample of the hero e-ink display.
3. A second meeting with a stated trigger, said by Oscar: "When I have a working hero unit in hand, may I show it to you?" The trigger is a thing, not a date.

Nothing with a number in it. No valuation, no round size, no equity, no price for the catalogue beyond the target price printed on each page.

## Five questions, in order
1. How did you come to invest, and what have you backed so far? (Opens her profile; also the safest opener per Holloway.)
2. When a European brand sources hardware in Shenzhen, what goes wrong first? (Her knowledge is the prize.)
3. Of these six to eight, which would you buy at the printed price, and which never? (Turns the catalogue into a demand signal instead of a pitch.)
4. If you wanted a sample of this one built next month, whom would you call? (The introduction, asked once.)
5. What would you need to see to take a second meeting? (Her trigger, which Oscar then matches or beats.)

## Not yet, whatever she asks
- Valuation, round size, instrument, equity split. Answer: "Too early for me to put a number on it; I want a unit in hand first."
- Fionn's role or share. He originated the idea with Oscar; until that is agreed between them, "the team" is Oscar and a friend who introduced the idea. Cofounder-agreement fork, own deep take.
- Design files, renders in editable form, supplier names Oscar already has. The design and the software are the only moat; the hardware is commodity. Show on screen, hand over nothing.
- Exclusivity of any kind, right of first refusal, "let me take this to my factory friends".
- The ARESA job beyond "I work in a shipyard and do this on my own time", until the contract check below is done.
- Pre-orders, ads, launch dates. Those are public acts and go through HQ's gate 3 anyway.

## Red flags (cofounder's opinion)
- She steers to valuation or equity in the first twenty minutes.
- She asks for exclusivity, a right of first refusal, or to "handle China" for a large stake.
- She wants to take the designs to her contacts without Oscar in the loop.
- She proposes that Oscar pay for samples or tooling through her contacts, or pay her a fee.
- She is vague about what she has invested in before, or Fionn cannot say either. Ask Fionn before the coffee.
- She wants a yes on anything at the table.
None of these ends the relationship; each one is a reason to slow down.

## ARESA contract checklist, before any money talk
Read the contract with these articles next to it. Fifteen minutes.

Employee inventions, Ley 24/2015 de Patentes:
- Art. 15: inventions that are the explicit or implicit object of the employment contract belong to the employer, with no extra pay unless the personal contribution clearly exceeds the contract ([art. 15](https://www.supercontable.com/informacion/ley_gestion/Articulo_15.Ley_24-2015-_de_24_de_julio-_de_Patentes..html)). Oscar's contract object is commercial strategy and market work at a shipyard, not consumer gadgets; check how the job description is worded.
- Art. 16: inventions outside art. 15.1 belong to the employee ([art. 16](https://www.supercontable.com/informacion/ley_gestion/Articulo_16.Ley_24-2015-_de_24_de_julio-_de_Patentes..html)).
- Art. 17: if the invention relates to the employee's work and company knowledge or means had a predominant influence, the employer may claim ownership or a right of use, against fair compensation ([art. 17](https://www.supercontable.com/informacion/ley_gestion/Articulo_17.Ley_24-2015-_de_24_de_julio-_de_Patentes..html)). Practical rule: no ARESA laptop, account, hours, suppliers or data touch the gadgets, ever. Keep the Saturday work on his own machine.
- Art. 18: for inventions under arts. 15 and 17 the employee must notify the employer in writing within one month of completing the invention; the employer has three months to claim ([art. 18](https://www.supercontable.com/informacion/ley_gestion/Articulo_18.Ley_24-2015-_de_24_de_julio-_de_Patentes..html)). If the gadgets are outside the contract object and made without company means, this duty does not arise; if in doubt, that is the lawyer question.
- Art. 19: a patent filed within one year after the employment ends is presumed made during it, unless proven otherwise; advance waivers by the employee are void ([art. 19](https://www.supercontable.com/informacion/ley_gestion/Articulo_19.Ley_24-2015-_de_24_de_julio-_de_Patentes..html)). So dated, private records of the design work (git history, dated files) matter.

Outside work and competition, Estatuto de los Trabajadores art. 21 ([text and versions](https://www.cuestioneslaborales.es/estatuto-trabajadores/articulo-21/)):
- Look for a pacto de plena dedicación (exclusivity). It is only valid with express economic compensation, and the employee can end it with 30 days' written notice, losing that compensation (art. 21.1 and 21.3).
- Look for a post-contract non-compete: at most two years for técnicos, six months for others, valid only with a real industrial or commercial interest of the employer and adequate compensation (art. 21.2). Consumer gadgets do not compete with a shipyard; the clause matters only if worded broadly.
- The page reports a new wording of art. 21.1 in force since 19 November 2026 (Real Decreto-ley 39/2026): the employer may not prohibit or limit work for other companies except for objective causes (safety, confidentiality, conflicts of interest), without prejudice to a paid exclusivity pact. The run read this on the linked page only and did not verify the BOE text; check it before relying on it.
- Also look for: a confidentiality clause (what counts as company information), any IP assignment clause wider than the law (art. 19.2 voids advance waivers of the employee's invention rights, but read it anyway), and any clause on holding shares or offices in other companies.

What to tell ARESA, if anything: nothing before the contract is read. If the contract is clean (no exclusivity, standard object), the cofounder's opinion is that a short written note to Oriol once a company exists is cheaper than a surprise later; that is a message to a real person and gets its own decision.
